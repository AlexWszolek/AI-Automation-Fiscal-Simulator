# Q&A ammunition — prepared answers for the room

One block per expected challenge. Each answer is fully sourced (documents in
`../sources/`); the presenter reads the short version, the long version is backup. These are
evidence blocks, not scripts — final wording is Alex's/the org's.

## 1. "Korea NEEDS automation — we have no workers."

**Short:** Correct, and it does not save the fiscal system. Automation substituting for
missing workers rescues *output*. Korean public finance is not funded by output — its largest
revenue source is contributions levied **per employed worker** (30.2% of all taxation, OECD
2025). Output rescued by capital is fiscally invisible to an earmarked, labour-levied fund.
Even the optimistic scenario breaks the fiscal path, because the plumbing is wired to labour.

**Backup:** The model deliberately does NOT simulate cancellation-vs-compounding — the two
act on different variables (output vs tax base) and do not net out. The declining-population
baseline is in the model for counterfactual correctness (Statistics Korea medium variant),
not as a cancellation mechanism.

## 2. "Exposure studies say white-collar; the observed effects hit young, low-skilled workers."

**Short:** Both are true and they are different measures. The OECD's Korea report finds
exactly this split. We do not use raw exposure: the model's input is the Bank of Korea's
complementarity-adjusted classification — only the high-exposure **low-complementarity**
share counts as displacement-prone. Professionals mostly land on the augmentation side;
clerical workers (17.4% of all employment) are the displacement epicentre.

**Backup:** BOK 이슈노트 2025-2 <그림 9> (aggregates 24% high-compl. / 27% low-compl.
published in text); IMF SIP 2025/013 Fig. 7 replicates it. Composition results are presented
as scenario arithmetic on this published classification, not as prediction.

## 3. "Your projections say the funds die anyway — what does AI add?"

**Short:** The deadlines are NABO's, not ours — health 2029/2031, pension 2065 post-reform.
The model adds only what arithmetic cannot: **how far automation moves those dates** (with
uncertainty bands), **what it takes back from the reform Korea just paid for** (0.3–1.6 of
the eight bought years), and **which institution bleeds first** (the statutory pension cap
routes high-wage damage to the general account and low-wage damage to the funds).

## 4. "Where do these numbers come from?"

**Short:** Every fund path is NABO's own publication — Focus 162 (health), the June-2025
pension-reform analysis 표 25 (pension), the mid-term projection via 「2026 대한민국 사회보험」
(employment insurance). The model erodes NABO's published paths; with zero automation it
reproduces them **identically** — that is an automated test, not a claim.

## 5. "Self-employment is a quarter of our economy — where is it?"

**Short:** Deliberately scoped out and disclosed: the model covers wage employees, ~76% of
employment. The self-employed appear qualitatively, and the direction is not comforting:
the sector is Korea's de facto old-age safety net (elderly poverty 38.1%, OECD's highest),
already saturated, ₩141tn in debt, and the segment where service automation is furthest
along. If it closes as a refuge, displaced and elderly workers land on Basic Pension and
NBLSS — the outlay side of the same budget.

## 6. "How wrong could the model be?"

**Short:** The biases run both directions and are printed, not hidden. The headline chain is
the assembled model — displacement is NET (re-employment into the finite service floor,
survivor raises, demand destruction) and EI benefit outlays are counted. Still understating
damage: NHI/NPS outlay responses and forgone investment income are ignored, and the AGI
scenarios carry only the cognitive channel (no published Korean robot-exposure vector — the
manual occupations' automation is absent from those rows). That is why every headline is
quoted as a range across adoption presets, revenue-share bands, and the figure-read error —
never a point.

## 7. "Why is the pension effect so small?" / "Why is the health effect so small?"

**Short (health):** Because the fund is nearly dead already — there is almost no runway left
to shorten. The sharper health expression is the post-depletion deficit widening.
**Short (pension):** It is not small: at central assumptions automation takes back roughly a
year (0.96–1.30) of the eight that a 44% contribution-rate increase — the first in 28 years,
18 years of deadlock — just bought; the fast worlds take back four to seven and a half. Priced
in reform-effort units, that is the point.
